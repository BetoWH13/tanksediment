Water Heater Maintenance Checklist

Published: 2024-12-30 · Safety review: 2026
This checklist is intentionally observation-first. It helps you notice changes and prepare for service. It does not instruct you to open electrical panels, work on gas components, operate pressure-relief hardware, or remove anode rods.

Things a homeowner can safely observe

CheckWhat to look forWhy it matters
LeaksWater around the tank, fittings, drain area, or nearby floor.Leaks can indicate a failing fitting, valve, or tank.
Rust or corrosionNew corrosion on the tank exterior or accessible connections.Corrosion can signal aging or water exposure that deserves evaluation.
Operating noiseNew rumbling, popping, hissing, or repeated cycling changes.Noise may be associated with sediment or another operating problem.
Hot-water performanceLess usable hot water, slower recovery, or inconsistent temperature.Performance changes help a technician narrow the cause.
Model and ageRecord the manufacturer, model number, serial number, and installation date if known.Maintenance instructions and expected service decisions vary by model and age.
Service historyNote the last known inspection, flush, repair, or component replacement.An unknown history can change whether disturbing old valves or fittings is sensible.

Maintenance items to verify from the manufacturer

Use the exact model number to locate the manufacturer's current maintenance instructions. Depending on the unit, those instructions may discuss draining or flushing, temperature settings, anode inspection, combustion service, or other periodic checks.

Do not substitute a generic internet interval or procedure for the instructions for your heater.

Tasks that may call for a qualified technician

  • Testing or replacing pressure-relief components.
  • Opening electrical panels or working on heating elements and wiring.
  • Gas-line, burner, combustion, pilot, or venting work.
  • Removing or replacing an anode rod.
  • Forcing, repairing, or replacing a stuck or leaking drain valve.
  • Flushing an older or deteriorated heater when the service history is unknown.
Stop and seek qualified help if you smell gas, see scorching or damaged wiring, find an active tank leak, see major corrosion, notice a bulging tank, or are unsure how the heater must be safely shut down and restarted.

How often should maintenance happen?

There is no single schedule that fits every water heater. Manufacturer instructions, water hardness, heater design, usage, age, and prior service all matter. A fixed annual flushing rule is too simplistic.

Useful questions for a service visit

  • What maintenance does this specific model require?
  • Is sediment likely to explain the symptoms I am seeing?
  • Is flushing appropriate given the tank's age and condition?
  • Are the drain and relief components in sound condition?
  • Is there any sign that replacement is more sensible than maintenance?

Our maintenance context tool can help organize dates, hardness context, and observed changes. It cannot inspect the heater, measure sediment, or determine whether a component is safe.
