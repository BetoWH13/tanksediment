The Science Behind Sediment Buildup

Published: 2024-12-30 · updated for clarity

Sediment in a water heater is not one single substance and hard water is not the only possible source. Mineral scale can form from dissolved calcium and magnesium, while sand, silt, corrosion products, and other debris can also contribute depending on the water supply and the condition of the system.

What heating changes

Heating can encourage some dissolved minerals, especially calcium compounds, to precipitate and form scale. The rate varies with water chemistry, temperature, usage, heater design, and treatment upstream of the heater.

Important distinction: A sediment filter removes suspended particles such as sand or silt. A water softener reduces hardness minerals. They solve different problems.

What buildup can affect

  • Heat transfer in parts of the heater where scale coats a heat-transfer surface.
  • Available tank volume when substantial material accumulates.
  • Noise or operating behavior in some heaters.
  • Valves and serviceability if deposits collect around openings.

Those effects do not translate into a universal percentage increase in energy use, a fixed loss of lifespan, or a guaranteed failure timeline. The actual impact depends on the appliance and the amount and type of buildup.

Hardness, scale, and maintenance

If you have hard water, confirm the level with a utility report or a hardness test rather than guessing from symptoms alone. Follow the water-heater manufacturer's maintenance guidance for your model. An older heater with an unknown service history, deteriorated valves, leaks, corrosion, gas concerns, or electrical faults should be assessed before maintenance is attempted.
